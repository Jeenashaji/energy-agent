# Why prices go negative

Short notes written for this project. Check details at smard.de.

Negative day-ahead prices occur when supply is higher than demand and some producers still keep
generating. Reasons include inflexible plants that are expensive to shut down and restart, and producers
whose income does not depend on the market price at that moment.

In Germany negative prices are most common around midday on sunny days with low demand, such as weekends
and public holidays in spring and summer, when solar output peaks. Evening hours, when solar output drops,
usually have higher prices.

Negative prices are a signal that flexibility is valuable: batteries, flexible demand (for example heat
pumps or electric vehicles) and exports can absorb the surplus. Support rules for newer renewable plants
in Germany reduce payments during negative-price periods, which encourages plants to curtail.
